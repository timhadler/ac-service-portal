Why yours

Product and hair, on a cold coil

Salons load units in a very specific way, and it is the combination that causes the trouble rather than either part on its own.

Hair and keratin dust

Fine cut hair stays airborne far longer than it looks like it should, and the heat pump is the thing in the room moving air.

Aerosol product

Hairspray, dry shampoo, lacquer and nail product settle on a cold coil and stay there. That film is what starts to smell when the unit kicks in.

A room people sit still in

A client in a chair for an hour with a unit blowing over them notices things a passing customer never would.

Your records

A dated record, if you keep one

Less formal than a clinic or a kitchen, but worth having — for your landlord, your insurer, or simply to know when it was last done.

What the record gives you

  • Date, technician and site. Sent the day the job is done.
  • Every unit, named by area. The floor, the basins, the treatment room.
  • Condition found and work done. Including product build-up and anything needing more than a clean.
  • One invoice for the salon. Simple as that.

Scope

What the clean covers

Short version, because there is not much to it.

We clean

  • Indoor units of every type. High-wall, ceiling cassette, ducted and floor console.
  • Coils, fans, filters and housings. Where the loading actually sits. Note that a full barrel fan clean is an upgraded clean, recommended per unit
  • Condensate trays and drains. The part that overflows in cooling mode if nobody has looked at it.
  • Outdoor units. Cleared, cleaned, and inspected so the system is not fighting itself.

We organise

  • Refrigeration work. Gas, regassing and mechanical repair are a different ticket, so we refer it to a qualified contractor and coordinate the visit.
  • Electrical work. Anything needing a registered electrician, the same way. One point of contact, rather than another job for you to project-manage.
How often

Six to twelve months, depending on what you use

How much you spray is the variable. A barber and a colour salon are not the same job.

Premises Interval Why
Hair salon 6–12 monthly Colour and finishing product plus cut hair. Sooner if you spray a lot or the floor is busy.
Barber Annual Plenty of hair, much less aerosol product. Usually fine at twelve months.
Beauty and nails 6–12 monthly Acetone, acrylic dust and aerosols, often in a smaller room with one unit doing all the work.
Massage and treatment Annual Light loading, but a room where smell matters more than most.

A starting interval based on how fast these premises load, not a rule. After the first clean we will tell you what yours needs.
