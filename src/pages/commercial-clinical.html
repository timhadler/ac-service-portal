Why yours

What actually builds up in a clinic

Clinical premises are cleaned constantly at surface level and almost never above head height. The unit is the one surface in the room nobody wipes down.

The surface nobody cleans

Your rooms get wiped between every patient. The vent grille three metres up does not, and it is moving air over everything in the room all day.

Vets carry a much heavier load

Hair and dander clog a filter and coat a coil far faster than anything in a human clinic. It is the single biggest difference between a vet surgery and a GP practice.

Condensate, in cooling season

In cooling mode a unit pulls a lot of water out of the air. Many clinic fitouts use a condensate pump rather than a gravity drain, and a blocked pump means an overflow — above a chair, a bench, or your ceiling tiles.

What the patient sees

A visibly grubby vent above someone lying in a chair for forty minutes does its own damage, whatever the coil is like.

How often a clinic needs it

Annual for most human clinical premises; twice a year where animals are involved.
