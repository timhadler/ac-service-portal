Why yours

Why a kitchen eats heat pumps

This is the fastest-loading category we service, and it is not close. It is also the one where the loading is genuinely invisible until it is severe.

Airborne grease

Cooking aerosols travel much further than the extraction canopy above the range. The heat pump pulls that air across a cold coil all service, and grease sticks to a cold coil.

Then it starts to smell

Grease on a coil goes rancid. When a unit smells on start-up in a food premises, that smell is going out over the dining room.

Visible mould on the vent

The thing a verifier notices without being asked, and the thing a customer photographs. By the time it is on the louvre it has been inside for a while.

Condensate over a prep bench

In cooling mode these units produce a lot of water. A blocked drain or condensate pump means an overflow, and in a kitchen that lands somewhere that matters.

How often

Six-monthly, and it is genuinely justified

This is the one premises type where we would push back on annual servicing. Grease loading is fast enough that twelve months is too long.

Premises Interval Why
Commercial kitchen 6-monthly Heaviest grease loading of anywhere we work. Units near the pass load fastest of all.
Restaurant dining room 6-monthly Cooking aerosols carry further than people expect, and a dining room unit runs all service.
Café 6-monthly Lighter than a full kitchen but still well above an office. Grills and toasters do more than you would think.
Pub and bar 6–12 monthly Depends on whether there is a working kitchen. Bar-only sites can usually run to twelve months.

A starting interval based on how fast these premises load, not a rule. After the first clean we will tell you what yours needs.
